Solar Battery Swapping for Heavy Freight

Warehouse rooftop solar charges spare truck packs at a swap station, and the trucks displace diesel. A kWh in a truck replaces 30 to 50c of diesel, compared with about 15c of retail power in a southern home or 5 to 9c wholesale. The model below costs the full chain and ranks candidate US sites on fuel prices, power prices, sunshine and the freight and charging infrastructure already there.

Method and sources

Diesel. EIA on-highway retail diesel, week of 5 Oct 2026: US $6.20, West Coast $7.23, Midwest $6.29, East Coast $5.95. Gulf Coast and Rocky Mountain values are estimates from their usual spread to the US average and are marked (est). A year earlier the US average was $3.72, so the scenario slider matters.

Electric trucks. About 1.9 kWh/mile for a loaded Class 8 tractor, in line with fleet reports of roughly 1.1 kWh/km. Cold climates add 5 to 12% for cabin and pack heating.

Batteries. BNEF 2025 survey: LFP packs averaged $81/kWh, with North America about 44% above China. The $150/kWh default adds a swappable enclosure, connectors and US pricing. Cycle life is not binding at these utilisations (about 270 full cycles a year over 8 years).

Swap stations. CATL's Qiji network reached about 305 heavy-truck stations in China in 2025 with a plan for about 900 more, and a UK joint venture with Octopus Energy targets first stations in 2027. US station capex is not public, so the $4M default is an assumption covering the robot, building, pack racks and grid connection.

Electricity. EIA 2024 industrial average prices by state. Rooftop LCOE default of 10c sits at the low end of Lazard's $88 to 197/MWh community and C&I range, because a single large warehouse roof with on-site use avoids wheeling and contract fragmentation. Solar is costed at a 1,400 kWh/kWp reference site and scaled by local yield.

Model. Delivered energy cost = solar share × rooftop LCOE + remainder × grid price, plus annualised packs (truck pack plus spares) and station capex, less VPP credit, all per kWh delivered. Per mile cost adds maintenance ($0.18 diesel, $0.12 electric), the annualised chassis premium and any road-use charge. Diesel prices already include about 60c/gal of fuel tax that electric trucks avoid until a road-use charge applies.

Infrastructure readiness. Scored 0 to 5 from: port or major intermodal terminal, existing or funded heavy-duty charging (WattEV Long Beach operating, Bakersfield, San Bernardino, Gardena, Sacramento and Blythe funded, TeraWatt I-10 corridor, NMDOT I-10 grant), large warehouse roof stock, short repeatable duty cycles, and grid headroom (sites competing with large data centre pipelines lose a point).

Indicative analysis for research purposes. Not investment advice.
